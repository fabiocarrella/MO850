MO850 – Tópicos Avançados em Ciência da Computação I

What We’ve Been Doing

  • We built a toolkit to measure psychological constructs in text
    • lexicons, embeddings, DDR/CCR, topic models
  • One shared assumption underlies all of it:
    • text is a trace of a human mind
  • Today we stress-test that assumption

The assumption

“If we can extract personality, morality, and emotion from text —
what happens when no one wrote it?”

  • LLM-generated text is now everywhere: ads, news, political messaging

  • Do our tools still mean what we think they mean?

Part 1 — From measurement to microtargeting

What we’ve already been doing, and why it matters

Simchon et al. (2023): text reveals personality

  • Reddit data (N = 1,105 writers + 10,050 readers)

  • Model 1: BERT predicts Big Five from produced text (r ≈ 0.33)

  • Model 2: BERT predicts personality from consumed text (r ≈ 0.13)

  • Key insight: linguistic style of what you write/read leaks your personality

→ This is our toolkit in action: embeddings measuring psychological constructs at scale

Simchon et al. (2024): the next step

What if you use a large language model to write ads?

  • Used GPT to generate political ads tailored to high/low openness

  • Personality-matched ads were significantly more persuasive

  • Combined with inferring personality from consumed text:

    • infer who you are → generate a message just for you → at scale

“A scalable manipulation machine that targets individuals based on
their unique vulnerabilities” — Simchon et al. (2024)

Carrella et al. (2025): warning does not help

If microtargeted ads are more persuasive, what if we just… tell people?

  • Three pre-registered studies (N = 666, 432, 669)

  • Compared targeted vs. non-targeted political ads, with and without warning popups

  • Result: targeted ads consistently more persuasive than non-targeted ones

  • Warning popups had no meaningful impact on persuasiveness

This is the break point

Our measurements were designed to understand human communication

LLMs flip the arrow:

Before After
measure personality in text generate text for a personality
extract moral framing inject moral framing on demand
detect emotion produce targeted emotional content

The question is no longer just measurement — it’s about what the text is doing

Part 2 — Are LLMs actually persuasive?

The empirical picture

What do we mean by persuasion?

  • Change in attitude, belief, or behavioral intention following exposure to a message

  • Classic framework: Elaboration Likelihood Model (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986)

    • Central route: deliberate processing of argument quality
    • Peripheral route: heuristics — source credibility, fluency, affect

  • Key question: which route do LLM messages exploit?

Goldstein et al. (2024) — propaganda at scale

  • Compared GPT-3-generated news articles to real Iranian/Russian propaganda (N = 8,221)

  • GPT-3 produced content as persuasive as real foreign propaganda

  • Human curation of prompts made it even more persuasive

  • Quality metrics (fluency, trustworthiness): GPT-3 matched or exceeded originals

  • The cost of producing convincing disinformation just dropped to near-zero

Bai, Willer et al. (2025) — policy attitudes

  • Three pre-registered experiments (total N = 4,829)

  • Topics: assault weapons ban, carbon tax, paid parental leave

  • LLM messages produced significant attitude change vs. neutral control

  • Overall: LLM messages as effective as human-written messages

But the why differed:

  • LLM persuasion ← perceived use of facts, logic, dispassionate voice
  • Human persuasion ← perceived originality and uniqueness

Salvi et al. (2025) — persuasion in conversation

What if LLMs don’t just send messages, but argue back?

  • Pre-registered debate experiment, N = 900

  • 2 × 2 × 3 design: GPT-4 vs. human opponent × with/without sociodemographic data × topic strength

  • GPT-4 without personalization: roughly equivalent to humans

  • GPT-4 with basic sociodemographic data: 81.2% higher odds of post-debate agreement

  • LLMs adapt in real time; the persuasive advantage scales with personalization

The meta-analytic picture

Hölbling, Maier & Feuerriegel (2025) — 7 studies, N = 17,422

  • Overall: no significant difference between LLMs and humans (g = 0.02, p = .53)

  • But: massive heterogeneity across studies (I² = 75.97%)

  • LLM model, conversation design, and topic domain together explain ~82% of variance

LLMs can match humans — but success depends heavily on context

Part 3 — How do LLMs persuade?

Linguistic complexity and moral language as mechanisms

Carrasco-Farré (2024) — cognitive effort as a route

Using “our” toolkit on LLM vs. human arguments (N = 1,251):

  • LLM arguments showed higher lexical and grammatical complexity

  • LLMs used moral foundations language more heavily (both positive and negative)

  • Emotional content (sentiment): no significant difference

  • LLMs push readers toward the central processing route —
    complexity signals depth, which readers interpret as substantiveness

In-class experiment

For each text:

  1. Rate:
    • persuasiveness
    • complexity
    • emotional intensity
  2. Guess:
    • Human or LLM?
  3. Briefly note:
    • what cue influenced your guess?

Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman (2023) - Why humans fail to detect AI text

  • Six experiments, N ≈ 4,600

  • People performed near chance when identifying AI vs. human text

  • Participants relied on flawed heuristics:

    • warmth/informality → “human”
    • formal/complex language → “AI”

  • LLMs can exploit these stereotypes by mimicking “human” cues

We may not detect authorship — only our expectations of what humans sound like

Zhou, He & Sun (2024) — the detection arms race

If humans can’t detect AI text, what about automated detectors?

  • Proposed ADAT: Adversarial Detection Attack on AI-Text
  • Small perturbations to AI-generated text (semantics preserved) designed to fool detectors
  • Two attack settings: white-box (attacker knows the model) and black-box (output only)
  • Result: detection models can be defeated in as little as 10 seconds
  • Iterative adversarial training improves robustness — but practical challenges remain

The heuristics humans use to spot AI text are flawed (Jakesch et al.) —
and the automated systems designed to replace those heuristics are equally fragile

Part 4 — The deception (and its escalation)

When LLMs don’t just persuade — they explain falsely

Danry et al. (2024) — deceptive explanations

  • Pre-registered experiment (N = 1,192, ~24,000 observations)
  • Compared: honest AI classification vs. deceptive AI classification with explanation
  • Result: deceptive AI with explanations significantly amplified belief in false headlines
  • More convincing than honest AI — and than simple misclassification
  • Key mechanism: the explanation heuristic

The explanation heuristic

  • People do not routinely scrutinize explanation content

  • The presence of an explanation signals authority and effort

  • This holds even when the explanation is logically invalid

    • (though invalid explanations were less effective than plausible-sounding ones)

  • cognitive reflection and AI skepticism did not protect participants

Why this matters for us

We use LLMs increasingly to:

  • annotate data

  • classify sentiment, morality, ideology

  • generate synthetic stimuli for experiments

If LLM explanations are systematically biased toward sounding authoritative —
our labels may be carrying persuasive structure we didn’t intend

  • If our annotation pipeline uses LLMs, are we measuring psychology or reproducing it?

Costello, Pennycook & Rand (2024) — AI reduces conspiracy beliefs

  • Participants engaged in short dialogues with GPT-4 about their own conspiracy beliefs

  • After the conversation:

    • conspiracy belief strength significantly decreased
    • effects persisted for at least 2 months

  • Reduction occurred even for highly entrenched beliefs

  • Key mechanism:

    • personalized, responsive counterargumentation
    • participants felt the AI understood and engaged with their reasoning

The same personalization mechanisms that amplify persuasion can also be used to reduce misinformation
